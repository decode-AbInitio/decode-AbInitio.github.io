01 THE MOTIVATION

The impending
opacity challenge.

Niraula et al., Nature Astronomy 6, 1287–1295 (2022)

Data quality now limits discovery

JWST measures exoplanet spectra with unprecedented precision, but the molecular opacities used to interpret them were built for earlier, less sensitive observations.

Inaccurate opacities bias results

When the opacity data fall short, retrieved abundances and temperatures can be biased, giving a confident but wrong picture of a planet.

A call for better data

The paper called for new, more accurate opacity data, including pressure broadening in hydrogen-rich atmospheres. DECODE was built to answer that call.

02 THE CHALLENGE

An atmosphere is only as legible as its data.

To interpret an exoplanet spectrum, researchers need to know how molecules absorb light under unfamiliar temperatures and pressures. Errors in the line-broadening coefficients propagate directly into the retrieved composition of a planet.

This is the impending opacity challenge identified by Niraula et al. (Nature Astronomy, 2022): JWST’s precision now exceeds the accuracy of the molecular opacity data used to interpret it, and DECODE was inspired by that finding. The figure shows how: broadening errors up to about 10% keep the retrieval bias below 1σ in most of the cases tested, while larger errors quickly bias the result. That 10% is the precision target DECODE’s calculations are built to meet.

03 THE APPROACH

Four steps from
quantum physics to data.

A full quantum scheme, benchmarked on CO₂ colliding with H₂ and He.

  1. 01

    Map the interaction

    Compute ab initio potential energy points for how the two molecules attract and repel.

  2. 02

    Fit a potential

    Turn the points into a smooth functional form that a scattering calculation can use.

  3. 03

    Solve the collision

    Solve the quantum dynamics to obtain the S matrix linking incoming and outgoing channels.

  4. 04

    Derive line parameters

    Build broadening cross sections and rate coefficients, and compare with laboratory data.

04 RESULTS

Calculated from first principles.
Checked against experiment.

Pressure-broadening coefficients γ for CO₂ colliding with He and H₂, across the rotational quantum number |m|.

CO₂–H₂

Calculations reach J = 24 and agree with the available measurements, with ortho- and para-H₂ treated separately.

CO₂–He

A second collision partner, built from a new potential with a T-shaped minimum at 4.80 Bohr and −47 cm⁻¹.

Padé fits

Smooth fits extend the results beyond the calculated range, ready for use in spectral models.

PROJECT LEAD / PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR

Iouli Gordon

Senior Physicist · Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian

Iouli Gordon leads the DECODE project and is one of the world’s foremost authorities on the molecular data that atmospheric science runs on. At the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory he heads the team that develops and maintains HITRAN, the international reference database of molecular absorption, along with its high-temperature companion HITEMP and the HAPI programming interface. These resources are used daily by researchers studying Earth’s atmosphere, planets, and exoplanets, and Gordon is lead author of the HITRAN2020 edition. DECODE extends that mission to the hot, hydrogen-rich worlds that JWST is now observing.

  • HITRAN & HITEMP
  • HAPI
  • Molecular line shapes
  • Principal Investigator, NASA XRP

EXOPLANET SCIENCE

Julien de Wit

Associate Professor · MIT

An exoplanet scientist in MIT’s Department of Earth, Atmospheric and Planetary Sciences, de Wit is a member of the team that discovered the seven Earth-sized planets of TRAPPIST-1 and works to characterize distant atmospheres with JWST. He brings the observer’s question to DECODE: which molecular data limit what we can learn from a spectrum, and how accurate must they be?

QUANTUM CALCULATIONS

Prajwal Niraula

Postdoctoral Fellow · MIT

Niraula is the postdoctoral fellow funded by DECODE, carrying out the ab initio quantum-dynamics calculations that turn interaction potentials into measurable broadening coefficients. He is lead author of the 2026 Astrophysical Journal paper on comprehensive ab initio computations of CO₂–H₂ and CO₂–He collisions, and coauthor of the 2025 paper that introduced the approach. He is also building the scattering workflow DECODE plans to release publicly, linking atomic-scale physics to planetary-scale spectra.

  • Quantum scattering
  • Scientific computing
  • CO₂–H₂ & CO₂–He
  • Lead author, ApJ 2026

THEORY & COLLISIONS

Laurent Wiesenfeld

Research Scientist · CNRS, Université Grenoble Alpes

A specialist in molecular collision dynamics at Grenoble’s Institute of Planetology and Astrophysics (IPAG), Wiesenfeld has spent his career computing how molecules collide in space. He is lead author of the 2025 paper that introduced the DECODE approach for CO₂ in hydrogen, coauthor of the 2026 follow-up covering CO₂–H₂ and CO₂–He, and his potential energy calculations are the foundation of the project’s work.
